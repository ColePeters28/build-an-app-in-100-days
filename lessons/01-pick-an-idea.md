# Day 1: How to pick an app idea people actually want

## The usual advice

Most people will tell you to pick an idea like this:

1. Find a problem.
2. Get people to pay for it.
3. Try it out on 10 people.

I think this is the wrong place to start. If nobody has built it yet, you're guessing whether anyone wants it.

## What I do instead

### 1. Find a crowded market

If tons of apps already do something, people already want it. You're not guessing.

Look at Bloom Energy drinks. They went up against Coca-Cola, Monster and other huge brands, in a market that looked full.
A full market means the customers are already there.

### 2. Find what's broken

You don't have to be better than your competitors at everything. You need one thing their users hate, or one thing they're missing.

For Pull Up, the crowded market was party apps like Posh and Partiful. What I thought was missing was a social side,
and a reason for people to go to parties and host them.

### 3. Pay the user back

People don't leave an app they already use just because yours is better. They want something for switching.

Pull Up's version is referral links. You share your link, and you earn a share of Pull Up's fee on tickets your friends buy.
You don't have to go to a party, subscribe to anything or pay anything.

## Your turn (15 minutes)

Write these down in one note. You'll use them on Day 2.

1. **Pick a crowded market.** Name a kind of app you already use where there are lots of choices.
   Write down 3 apps in it.
2. **Find what's broken.** Read the 1-star and 2-star reviews of those 3 apps on the App Store.
   Write down the complaint you see most often.
3. **Pay the user back.** Write one thing your app could give someone for switching:
   money, time saved, status, or something free that the others charge for.
4. **Say it in one sentence:** "It's like ___, but ___, and you get ___."

If you can't fill in the sentence, pick a different market and go again.
